With AI, I created Quillmere Systems, a fictional B2B SaaS company with a complete set of mock books: monthly GL actuals, an operating plan, a headcount roster and an ARR bridge.

An AI plays my VP of Finance. It hands out assignments that follow a real fiscal year (month-end variance, Q4 reforecast, next year's budget, the board deck) and reviews what I turn in. I take on the role of the FP&A analyst, and the analysis, models and write-ups are mine. Each entry shows the ask, my approach, what I found, and the review it got.

    Investment research

    Nebius Group: Buy, $60. Target hit.

    Initiation built at Romero Capital in July 2025: diligence, a 10-year three-statement model, DCF and comps, and a written thesis across 28 slides.

    During my internship at Romero Capital, I built full investment cases from scratch: company diligence, financial modeling, DCF and comps valuation, and a written investment thesis. Below is my analysis of Nebius Group (NBIS), an AI infrastructure company I covered in July 2025. I issued a Buy rating at $60.00 (~15.5% upside) on July 21, 2025, with an 8 to 16 month window. The stock first closed above the target on August 7, 2025, 17 days later.

    TickerNBISNasdaqGS

    The thesis

    1. Hypergrowth at the top line. Q1 2025 revenue of $55.3M, up ~385% YoY, with an ARR target of $750M–$1B by the end of 2025.
    2. A war chest, not a debt load. ~$1.4B in cash against ~$202M of debt funds a ~$2B 2025 CapEx buildout without dilution.
    3. A moat made of GPUs. Scarce access to NVIDIA capacity and heavy capital requirements keep new entrants out.
    4. Losses narrowing. Adjusted EBITDA loss improved to ($62.6M) in Q1 2025 from ($70.9M), with breakeven guided for late 2025.

    About

    The translation layer

    B.S. Finance, Minor in Fintech. University of Delaware, Lerner College of Business & Economics, 2026.

    I studied Finance at the University of Delaware's Lerner College of Business with a minor in Fintech, and honestly the minor felt more like the point than an add-on. I've always been more interested in the places where finance and technology run into each other than in keeping them separate.

    Over the past few years I've gotten comfortable with the full range of what a finance role can look like today: building models from scratch, working through earnings calls and SEC filings, writing investment memos, and more recently, building the kinds of tools that automate the parts of that work that don't need to be manual. Automation, data pipelines, and applied AI are where I think the next generation of finance professionals will separate themselves, and that's where I'm putting my energy.

    What actually draws me to FP&A and corporate finance is the translation layer. Taking a messy set of numbers and turning it into something a leadership team can make a real decision with is genuinely interesting to me. I want to be in roles where finance has a seat at the strategy table, not just in the back office processing reports.

    I built my first computer at 12 just by watching YouTube videos and figuring it out. That instinct hasn't really changed. If I don't understand how something works, I want to take it apart until I do, and then usually try to make it better.
