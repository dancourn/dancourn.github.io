September Close: Budget vs. Actual Variance

The ask

September operating income came in well below plan. Tell me why, how much of it fixes itself, and what to worry about for Q4. I need a budget-vs-actual P&L built off the GL, one page of commentary for the CFO, and 3 to 5 flags for the Q4 reforecast.Rachel Okafor, VP of Finance (fictional, AI-simulated)

How I built it

  1. Built the P&L off the raw ledger. SUMIFS on the GL and the budget for September and year-to-date, with a single month cell that rolls the whole table forward.
  2. Proved it ties. A check that subtracts every GL row from my P&L lines and returns zero, so nothing is missed or double counted.
  3. Found what's material. Split the P&L into 48 department/account lines and flagged the ones over $50K and 10%.
  4. Traced each one to the journal entries. Read the vendor and description behind every flagged line and tagged it timing, one-time or run-rate.
  5. Sized the Q4 impact. Put a dollar figure, and an owner where action was needed, on everything that carries forward.

What I found

82%

of the September miss fixes itself

An expo budgeted for October that ran in September, two implementation milestones that slipped a month or two, and one-time litigation fees.

$1.3M

behind plan, hidden in a "flat" YTD

Year-to-date looked on plan only because R&D was $1.35M under budget on unfilled roles. As those roles fill, the cushion goes away.

$350K

of Q4 revenue lost, below the threshold

A customer worth $1.4M ARR left on 9/1. Subscription revenue only missed by 0.7%, so the materiality filter alone would have missed it.

My Q4 reforecast flags

  1. Cut October Events to ~$55K, not $420K. The expo is already paid for; budgeting it again double counts ~$365K.
  2. Lower Q4 subscription revenue by ~$350K for the lost customer, and lower the year-end ARR forecast with it.
  3. Renew the cloud reserved instances this month (Engineering and Procurement) to stop ~$45K a month of avoidable cost.
  4. Plan for ~$255K of extra Q4 commissions if bookings keep beating plan; the budget funds $0 for accelerators.

What I learned

  • A check should return zero. A total proves nothing; a difference of $0 proves every dollar is accounted for.
  • Timing vs. one-time is one question: was it in the budget, just in another month?
  • Look below the threshold. Big items can hide inside a line that nets out small.
  • Hiring-lag savings don't come back. When roles fill, the cushion stops growing; it doesn't refill.
  • Raise data conflicts early. I found two ARR targets in the source data and logged it as a question for my manager.

Where this shows up on the job

  • Monthly close and the flash report: the budget-vs-actual package every FP&A team sends each month.
  • Reforecasting: turning "what happened" into "what changes" for the rest of the year.
  • Controls: building models that tie to the ledger so leadership can trust the numbers.
  • Executive writing: leading with the bottom line and putting a dollar figure on every point.
